The flow

5 steps

Markdown gets you a wall of text someone has to read top-to-bottom. A page gets you something shaped — typography, layout, hover states. The skill bridges the two. Here's what happens between "make me a page" and the link landing in your hand.

01 · Brief

You hand me an idea

A topic, a brainstorm, a thread. Concrete enough that I can name an audience and three facts.

→
02 · Slug

I pick a kebab-case slug

Six words or fewer, derived from the topic. The URL you'll share is built from it.

→
03 · Render

One file, ivory and clay

Tokens copied verbatim. Serif headlines, mono eyebrows, SVG that obeys the class system.

→
04 · Publish

publish.sh commits and pushes

Index regenerates with a card for the new page. GitHub Pages picks up the change in a minute or two.

→
05 · Link

You get a URL back

Forward it to anyone. They open it; the typography does the rest.

03

Why every page looks the same

10 rules

The look isn't taste — it's a checklist. The skill validates each item before the script will publish. Removing any one of them breaks the family resemblance, so they're not optional.

  • One file. No <link>, no <script src>.
  • <title>, description, vivid:created in <head>.
  • Palette is the seven warm tokens — ivory, clay, oat, olive, slate, plus three grays.
  • Serif for h1/h2/titles. Sans for body. Mono for eyebrows and labels.
  • h1 uses clamp(38px, 5.4vw, 62px) with one italic clay <em>.
  • Mono labels are uppercase, 11–13px, with letter-spacing 0.08–0.12em.
  • Container .wrap caps at 1120px with 32px horizontal padding.
  • SVG uses the class system: .st .fl .cl .ol .oa .sl .wh .ln .lc .da.
  • Hover lifts cards by translateY(-3px) with a 150ms transition.
  • Footer humanizes: it acknowledges the page is itself the artifact.
